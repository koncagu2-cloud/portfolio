Apple Ring

What happens when your watch comes off?

When Watch charges overnight, Ring could supply a morning estimate. Showing its source and withholding incomplete readings may help people trust the companion app. That value remains untested.

01 / Demonstrate the Partnership

Show the partnership. Don’t just name it.

The earlier pairing section changed a source label next to a fixed ecosystem image. It described two devices without demonstrating what someone gained from using them together.

“Charge Watch. Keep your night.” gave the proposal a specific moment. The coded interaction moves from Watch charging overnight to a Ring summary on Watch in the morning, with Ring identified as the source and sync routed through iPhone.

Ring-only mode changes the companion to iPhone, keeping Watch optional. The summary labels its last-sync time instead of implying live information.

02 / The Question

Why a ring, if you already have Watch?

The project began as an Apple-inspired launch page. As it developed, visual polish exposed a more important gap — the concept needed a reason to exist alongside Apple Watch.

The working hypothesis became more specific. Some Watch owners might want to remove it overnight while retaining a limited record of their night. Ring could be an optional source during those hours, with the result available on Watch the next morning.

This is a design hypothesis, not a research finding. Someone happy wearing Watch overnight may have little reason to add Ring.

03 / Beyond the Ideal State

A gap should look like a gap.

Missing readings and a disconnected Ring have different meanings. The prototype makes that distinction visible instead of showing a plausible-looking number in both cases.

An incomplete night has no complete sleep estimate. A disconnected Ring shows no new sync and withholds the current battery percentage. These captures document working prototype states rather than a real device connection.

Illustrative Watch companion states. No real health information or hardware connection is used.

04 / Question the Physical Story

A beautiful image still has to make sense.

A charging render showed Ring upright beside a vertical post, with no convincing alignment or support. The image suggested a finished mechanism that had not been designed.

The revised pose places Ring horizontally on a supporting dock around a centered insert. It improves the physical explanation without proving the charging circuit.

The five-day battery figure is explicitly a provisional target. Coil placement, metal heating, antenna performance, capacity, and charging time remain unresolved.

05 / Preserve the Journey

A choice should survive the next screen.

Closing and reopening the viewer discarded a chosen finish. The revision connects the viewer and page through shared in-memory preferences for this visit.

Reset restores the sample examples while preserving display preferences. These are demonstration settings, not device permissions; no personal readings are stored or transmitted.

Verified return-path defect and subsequent shared-state revision, documented with the retained prototype source and regression checks.

06 / Implementation & Contribution

Designed in detail. Built to explore.

The result is a responsive product story with finish selection, shared display preferences, and a working handoff demonstration. I directed the concept and revisions; Codex assisted with strategy, writing, implementation, and checks. Product stills are AI-generated.

Implementation boundaries

The bundled prototype runs in the browser. No native Watch app, real device connection, or Apple Health integration is implemented.

Front-end structure and access

HTML, CSS, and JavaScript support the product story and shared state. The procedural Three.js viewer loads on demand, with a static fallback.

Explicit controls accompany dragging; the prototype also includes visible focus, reduced-motion alternatives, and mobile result visibility.

Implementation and contribution described from the retained prototype source and project decision notes.

07 / Evidence & Next Steps

The next test is the reason to care.

The web implementation makes the hypothesis inspectable; it does not establish demand. Recorded checks below are implementation QA, not participant research or proof of hardware feasibility.

A usability protocol is prepared. The next step is to ask Watch owners to explain the partnership and explore missing and disconnected states. If the concept offers too little beyond wearing Watch overnight, the product direction should change.
