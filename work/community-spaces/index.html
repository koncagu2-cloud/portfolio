Community Spaces

From finding a space to joining an event.

Design participation as one connected journey, from the physical space to discovering and joining an event. I worked across our research, survey, spatial concepts, and prototype, helping put practical details before commitment.

The project in brief

The problem

A place to gather needs a way to participate. The team’s research connected shared-space needs with finding activities on specific dates.

Our choice

Pair the physical-space proposal with KIBS, an event-discovery app concept. Put event details and participation requirements between browsing and registration so visitors can plan before committing.

About the original screens and this walkthrough

Project status. University concept and prototype; not launched. Usability evaluation is proposed. Research findings are reported from the class project. The portfolio walkthrough presents original interface states, not verified prototype connections or working registration. Dates, counts, and availability in the original screens are illustrative.

Design decisions

  1. Connect the place and the task ↓

    Shared-space needs inform spatial sketches; KIBS offers a path from event discovery to a proposed visit.

  2. Put planning information before signup ↓

    Date-specific needs connect to event dates, details, and registration requirements. Comprehension has not been tested.

  3. Retain structure as fidelity increases ↓

    Repeated event rows persist from wireframe to high fidelity; imagery and color add character without replacing the browsing task.

01 / The Challenge

A place to gather. A way to join in.

The team’s documented needs link study and relationship-building with information about events on specific dates. The proposal pairs a shared physical space with KIBS, an event-discovery and registration app concept.

The central digital task is finding an event and signing up. The design needs to explain what, where, and when before someone plans a visit; access to the physical space is part of that wider experience.

02 / Needs & Questions

What the project evidence supports.

The project materials document two needs and one task: a place to study and connect, date-specific event information, and a path to sign up. This section traces how the concept responds and what still needs evaluation; the materials do not show whether those needs were met.

How we worked. We carried out the research and survey together, explored the concept as a team, and developed the prototype collaboratively. The connections below explain how the documented needs relate to our designs.

04 / See confirmation

Understand the acknowledgment.

User task. Understand the acknowledgment and available next actions.

Design response. The concept presents registration confirmation and calendar/home actions.

Does the wording communicate what happened and what to do next? This is an original screen, not an operating registration result.

Show design annotations

04 / The tradeoff

The extra review step favors an informed commitment over the shortest signup path. Capacity, eligibility and cancellation behavior still need to be designed and evaluated. The original “Continue Shopping” confirmation label also needs a clearer fit with event registration.

06 / Reflection & Next Steps

Designing beyond the screen.

The result is a shared-space proposal, spatial sketches, and KIBS interface states from event discovery to confirmation. The service was not launched, and no attendance or usability improvement is established.

Looking back, I would focus on the connection between registration and the actual visit. Clearer confirmation wording, capacity information and physical-access details would make that wider experience more complete. These are my current refinement priorities, not changes tested during the original project.

Validate the journey

Test event discovery, registration, and confirmation with potential users.

Evaluate accessibility

Review navigation, contrast, screen-reader support, and interaction targets.

Explore real-world scenarios

Consider canceled events, capacity limits, and whether users should be able to browse without logging in.

The confirmation concept includes a “Continue Shopping” action. Future evaluation should examine whether that wording fits event registration and makes the next step clear.
