Quick read / Design rationale

Three decisions to explore.

  1. Connect the place and the task ↓

    Shared-space needs inform spatial sketches; KIBS offers a path from event discovery to a proposed visit.

  2. Put planning information before signup ↓

    Date-specific needs connect to event dates, details, and registration requirements. Comprehension has not been tested.

  3. Retain structure as fidelity increases ↓

    Repeated event rows persist from wireframe to high fidelity; imagery and color add character without replacing the browsing task.

01 / The Challenge

A place to gather. A way to join in.

The team’s documented needs link study and relationship-building with information about events on specific dates. The proposal pairs a shared physical space with KIBS, an event-discovery and registration app concept.

The central digital task is finding an event and signing up. The design needs to explain what, where, and when before someone plans a visit; access to the physical space is part of that wider experience.

Project brief and need statements · Class presentation, pages 2, 4, 6, and 16; original Figma user needs.

06 / Reflection & Next Steps

Designing beyond the screen.

The result is a shared-space proposal, spatial sketches, and KIBS interface states from event discovery to confirmation. The service was not launched, and no attendance or usability improvement is established.

The relationship between space and screen is the central lesson: registration alone does not explain the whole visit. The next review should connect event information, eligibility, capacity, and physical access. These are proposed next steps, not completed testing.

Validate the journey

Test event discovery, registration, and confirmation with potential users.

Evaluate accessibility

Review navigation, contrast, screen-reader support, and interaction targets.

Explore real-world scenarios

Consider canceled events, capacity limits, and whether users should be able to browse without logging in.

The confirmation concept includes a “Continue Shopping” action. Future evaluation should examine whether that wording fits event registration and makes the next step clear.
