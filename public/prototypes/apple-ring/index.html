A gap stays a gap.

Ring supplies the night. Watch supplies daytime activity. The proposed shared record keeps sources visible and avoids adding duplicate readings.

What would work together?

Ring’s available sleep estimate and last-synced battery status would appear on Watch after syncing through iPhone. Watch would remain the device for workouts and on-wrist interaction. A shared record would identify a source for each metric and interval, rather than add overlapping counts.

Ring can also be used with iPhone alone. It would not replace Watch’s GPS, ECG, calling, or workout controls. Missing or unsynced readings would stay visible as gaps; Ring would not recreate Watch-only measurements.

Device coordination, source reconciliation, and Watch integration are proposals. This prototype does not connect devices or write to Apple Health, Vitals, or Activity.

Time away from the charger.

5 daysProvisional battery-life target

The goal: several nights and days between charges.

This is a design target, not measured performance. Ring size, sensing frequency, wireless use, and battery age would affect the result. Charging time has not been established.

Inside the idea.

Each part has a purpose.

Proposed hardware architecture. Component selection, dimensions, and performance remain unvalidated.

01 Shell and liner
The titanium exterior provides the proposed structure. An electrically insulating inner liner separates the electronics from the finger, with clear windows for optical sensing.
02 Optical sensing
Light emitters and a detector behind the inner windows would sample reflected light to estimate pulse. The render’s three windows indicate an arrangement study, not a finalized sensor layout.
03 Movement and interpretation
A proposed motion sensor would record movement. Sleep estimates would combine movement and pulse patterns; they would not directly measure how rested you feel.
04 Power and connection
A rechargeable cell, processor, memory, and Bluetooth Low Energy radio would share the band. The proposed phone connection would sync readings in batches, rather than stream continuously.
How would the dock charge the ring?

The ring rests on the dock’s seating surface, around an insert matched to its size. The proposal transfers power inductively through the nonmetallic inner liner to a receiver inside the band. It does not require a hole in the outer shell or exposed charging contacts.

The dock would use USB-C power. Its proposed light would indicate charging; iPhone would show a text status and battery percentage after connection. The render illustrates placement, not a proven charging circuit or MagSafe compatibility.

Coil alignment, electrical isolation, metal heating, and charging cutoffs would need engineering tests before this arrangement could be considered viable.

What is the five-day target based on?

The intended use is overnight sleep estimation and periodic daytime sensing, with motion detection and occasional phone sync. The concept has no display, cellular radio, or GPS. Those choices limit its scope; they do not prove a runtime.

A five-day target means 120 hours. A selected battery would need to supply that workload with room for conversion losses, a reserve, and aging. No battery capacity or power measurements have been established, so this target could change after testing, particularly for smaller sizes.

What still needs to be resolved?

Battery packaging, charging-coil and antenna placement, wireless performance through the proposed shell, heat, sealing, skin-contact materials, and comfortable sizing. The continuous metal exterior may need revision if it blocks power transfer or the radio signal.

When the ring is off the finger or out of power, that interval should remain a gap in the record. Water resistance, serviceability, charging speed, and sensing accuracy are not specified or verified.
